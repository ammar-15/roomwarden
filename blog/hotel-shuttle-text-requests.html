How to run a hotel shuttle without the radio

By AmmarOctober 8, 20263 min read

Short answerThe simplest way to run a hotel shuttle is to let guests request rides by text and have those requests show up for the front desk and driver at the same time. Guests get a reply with a pickup time, and every trip gets logged.

At a lot of hotels, a shuttle request goes like this. The guest calls the front desk. The agent puts them on hold, radios the driver, waits, maybe radios again, then gets back to the guest with a rough time. If the desk is busy, that call waits too. Multiply that by every flight and it adds up fast.

Where it breaks down

  • The front desk becomes the middleman for every ride, even when there's a line at check-in.
  • The driver can't always answer the radio while driving.
  • Guests don't get a clear pickup time, so they call again.
  • Nothing gets written down, so nobody knows how many rides happen or when the busy times are.

A better setup: requests by text

Give guests one number to text for a ride. They send where they're going and when, and the request lands in front of the front desk and the driver at the same time. Whoever's free confirms it, and the guest gets a text back with the pickup time. When the driver's on the way, the guest can get another text.

Guests don't need an app, and most of them would rather text than call anyway. The desk stops relaying messages, and the driver sees the full list of upcoming pickups in one place.

Log every ride

Once rides go through one system, you get a record for free. You can see which hours are busiest, how many airport runs happen per day and whether you need a second driver on certain shifts. That's hard to figure out from radio chatter.

How Room Warden handles it
